# CS 250: Software Development Lifecycle

This repository contains my **Sprint Review and Retrospective** from CS 250: Software Development Lifecycle. This project demonstrates what I learned about Agile development, the Scrum framework, and the importance of communication and collaboration throughout the software development life cycle.

## How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating user stories help with this?

I interpret user needs by first understanding what the user is trying to accomplish instead of immediately focusing on how I am going to code the solution. Creating user stories helps turn those needs into clear requirements by describing a feature from the user's perspective and explaining why that feature is valuable. During the SNHU Travel project, I learned that user stories give the Product Owner, developers, and testers a shared understanding of what needs to be created. Combining user stories with clear acceptance criteria also makes it easier to determine whether the finished feature actually meets the user's expectations.

## How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?

I approach developing programs by breaking larger problems into smaller, manageable tasks and making sure I understand the requirements before changing or writing code. This course showed me that software development does not always follow a perfect plan from beginning to end. Requirements can change, unexpected problems can occur, and customer feedback may change the direction of a project. In my future development work, I would like to incorporate Agile practices such as user stories, prioritized product backlogs, regular team communication, continuous testing, Sprint Reviews, and Sprint Retrospectives. I especially like developing and testing in smaller increments because it gives the team opportunities to identify problems and make improvements throughout development instead of discovering them at the end.

## What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

To me, being a good team member in software development means communicating clearly, taking responsibility for my work, and being willing to collaborate with others. A good team member should ask questions when requirements are unclear, share progress and challenges, accept feedback, and help other members of the team when possible. One thing that stood out to me during this course was the importance of avoiding knowledge silos where only one person understands a certain part of the software. Sharing knowledge and communicating consistently makes the entire team stronger and prevents the success of a project from depending on one person. Being a good team member means completing my individual responsibilities while also contributing to the success of the team as a whole.
